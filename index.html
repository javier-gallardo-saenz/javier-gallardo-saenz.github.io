About

I am a first-year PhD student in Mathematics at the University of British Columbia, supervised by Christoph Ortner. My research is on mathematics and machine learning for atomistic simulation, and my first projects are on problems in electronic structure.

I trained in both mathematics and physics: a dual degree in Mathematics and Engineering Physics at CFIS (UPC), followed by the COSSE joint MSc in computational science at TU Delft and KTH. Since then I have worked on diffusion models for protein–ligand docking (published in Nature Machine Intelligence), molecular-dynamics-assisted hierarchical learning for siRNA therapeutics at AstraZeneca, and stability theory for constrained-optimization dynamics at UC San Diego. Across these projects I build ML models for molecular systems that respect the underlying physics and that I can analyze mathematically.

Theses

MSc thesis · KTH & TU Delft · AstraZeneca Molecular AI · 2026

Molecular-dynamics-assisted hierarchical machine learning for modified siRNA activity prediction

Supervisors: Emilie Gerard, Marco Klähn, Olof Runborg · Awarded the highest grade

  • Curated a dataset of more than 88,000 experimentally characterized chemically modified siRNAs, and built an automated fragmentation pipeline that maps each modified nucleotide to force-field parametrizations (modXNA).
  • Built a high-throughput molecular dynamics pipeline (GROMACS, AMBER force fields) for ~6,000 modified trimers. It extracts structural and dynamical descriptors such as helical parameters, sugar puckers, hydrogen bonds and interaction energies.
  • Framed activity prediction as learning on a graph of graphs, and proved that standard message-passing networks suffer exponential over-squashing on siRNA duplexes, even when hydrogen bonds and base stacking are encoded as edges.
  • Introduced modular, airtight hierarchical architectures that combine atom, nucleotide, trimer and molecule levels. On a generalization-oriented split they clearly outperform the previous best baseline, and ablations show that the MD-derived features contribute measurably.

BSc thesis · UPC · carried out at UC San Diego · 2024

Extending input-to-state stability to closed sets: applications to robustness of anytime constrained optimization

Supervisor: Jorge Cortés (UCSD) · Tutor: Carles Batlle (UPC) · Awarded the highest grade

  • Introduced Set Input-to-State Stability (SISS) for flows on closed sets with compact sets of equilibria, gave sufficient conditions for it to hold, and characterized how it relates to ISS and local ISS.
  • Proved that, under reasonable assumptions, both the continuous and the discretized Safe Gradient Flow are SISS. This gives robustness guarantees for anytime-safe constrained optimization under disturbances.

Projects

e3nn.jl: IrrepsArray

Julia · E(3)-equivariant neural networks

Contributions to e3nn.jl, a Julia port of e3nn. I implemented the IrrepsArray data structure, which pairs feature arrays with their O(3) irreducible-representation layout. It supports construction from irreps strings, chunking by irrep, type conversion and zero-extension, following the core abstraction of e3nn-jax.

Hub-Laplacian operators for directional GNNs

Python · PyTorch Geometric · QM9 · with A. Carraro and I. Heijmink (TU Delft)

Integrated degree-biased graph shift operators, fixed or with learnable bias α, into polynomial-filter GCNNs and Graph Anisotropic Diffusion for molecular property regression on QM9. With a well-tuned α they consistently beat standard baselines, which we link to larger spectral gaps. Report.

Chemical potential of a dense Lennard-Jones fluid

Julia · molecular dynamics · free-energy methods

Alchemical particle insertion with a soft-core potential, using Langevin NVT dynamics with a BAOAB integrator. It compares Widom insertion, thermodynamic integration, FEP, BAR and MBAR from the gas phase up to high-density fluids, with uncertainty estimates and tail corrections. Report.

Opinion inference on complex networks

Python · NetworkX

Simulates opinion dynamics on random graph models (Erdős–Rényi, Barabási–Albert, configuration, stochastic block and hierarchical models) and compares methods for inferring a node's state from partial, local observations of its neighborhood.

About

I am a first-year PhD student in Mathematics at the University of British Columbia, supervised by Christoph Ortner. My research is on mathematics and machine learning for atomistic simulation, and my first projects are on problems in electronic structure.

I trained in both mathematics and physics. At CFIS (UPC) I completed a double degree, earning a BSc in Mathematics and a BSc in Engineering Physics simultaneously. I then did the COSSE joint MSc in computational science at TU Delft and KTH. Since then I have worked on diffusion models for protein–ligand docking (published in Nature Machine Intelligence), molecular-dynamics-assisted hierarchical learning for siRNA therapeutics at AstraZeneca, and stability theory for constrained-optimization dynamics at UC San Diego. My goal is to build ML models for atomistic and molecular systems that respect the underlying physics, and to analyze them mathematically.

Publications

  1. Informed protein–ligand docking via geodesic guidance in translational, rotational and torsional spaces

    R. Miñán, J. Gallardo, Á. Ciudad, A. Molina · Nature Machine Intelligence 7, 2025

    Paper · Slides

  2. GeoDirDock: Guiding Docking Along Geodesic Paths

    R. Miñán, J. Gallardo, Á. Ciudad, A. Molina · GEM Bio Workshop, ICLR 2024

    arXiv

Both papers guide the reverse process of a diffusion model along geodesics in the product of translational, rotational (SO(3)) and torsional spaces. This steers blind docking toward expert-specified binding regions and improves both RMSD accuracy and the physical plausibility of the predicted poses.

I devised this method three years ago, and I now understand its underlying mathematics much better. My presentation slides cover it more carefully: score-based diffusion on the product manifold 𝕋(3) × SO(3) × SO(2)m, and geodesic guidance through its exponential and logarithm maps.

Theses

MSc thesis · KTH & TU Delft · AstraZeneca Molecular AI · 2026

Molecular-dynamics-assisted hierarchical machine learning for modified siRNA activity prediction

Supervisors: Emilie Gerard, Marco Klähn, Olof Runborg · Awarded the highest grade

Can the gene-silencing activity of a chemically modified siRNA be predicted from simulations of its short building blocks? I built both the simulation infrastructure and the models to test this. The resulting hierarchical model clearly outperforms the best previously available model for this task.

  • Fragmentation and MD workflow. I designed and built an end-to-end, incremental workflow from siRNA sequences to MD features. It starts with an automated fragmentation procedure that splits each modified nucleotide into backbone, sugar and base fragments. It then matches these against the modXNA parametrization catalogue to find the unique trimers that can be simulated, across a dataset of more than 88,000 experimentally measured siRNAs. Next, the workflow prepares and runs the GROMACS simulations and extracts descriptors such as helical parameters, sugar puckers, hydrogen bonds and interaction energies. It produced a new dataset of MD-derived properties for nearly 6,000 modified trimers. As new siRNAs arrive, only previously unseen trimers are simulated.
  • Multi-cluster scheduling. The workflow includes a scheduler that treats job allocation as a mixed-integer linear program. It distributes about 29,000 simulations across heterogeneous CPU and GPU SLURM partitions on two clusters, in Sweden and the US, to minimize total wall-clock time, and it re-optimizes when compute availability changes. Other departments at AstraZeneca now use the workflow.
  • Theory. I framed activity prediction as learning on a graph of graphs. I then proved that standard message-passing networks suffer exponential over-squashing on siRNA duplexes, even when hydrogen bonds and base stacking are encoded as edges.
  • Models and results. The modular, airtight hierarchical architectures combine atom, nucleotide, trimer and molecule levels, and inject the MD features at the trimer level. On a generalization-oriented split, the best model lowers the test MAE from 0.230 to 0.175 (about 24%) relative to Cm-siRPred, the strongest published model, with roughly 24× fewer parameters. It also beats a tuned XGBoost baseline, and ablations show that the MD-derived features contribute measurably.

BSc thesis · UPC · carried out at UC San Diego · 2024

Extending input-to-state stability to closed sets: applications to robustness of anytime constrained optimization

Supervisor: Jorge Cortés (UCSD) · Tutor: Carles Batlle (UPC) · Awarded the highest grade

  • Introduced Set Input-to-State Stability (SISS) for flows on closed sets with compact sets of equilibria, gave sufficient conditions for it to hold, and characterized how it relates to ISS and local ISS.
  • Proved that, under reasonable assumptions, both the continuous and the discretized Safe Gradient Flow are SISS. This gives robustness guarantees for anytime-safe constrained optimization under disturbances.

Projects

e3nn.jl: IrrepsArray

Julia · E(3)-equivariant neural networks

Contributions to e3nn.jl, a Julia port of e3nn. I implemented the IrrepsArray data structure, which pairs feature arrays with their O(3) irreducible-representation layout. It supports construction from irreps strings, chunking by irrep, type conversion and zero-extension, following the core abstraction of e3nn-jax.

Eigenvector embeddings and spectral bias in PINNs

Python · JAX · NGSolve · with A. Carraro · TU Delft, course taught by Alexander Heinlein

Studied whether adding graph-Laplacian or Laplace–Beltrami eigenfunctions to a PINN's inputs helps it solve Poisson problems on non-convex domains (C-shaped, dumbbell and spiral), using finite-element solutions (Netgen/NGSolve, PINVIT eigensolver) as ground truth. The embeddings gave at most modest gains. Projecting the learned solutions onto the Laplace–Beltrami eigenbasis showed why: the networks consistently fail to capture the lowest eigenmode. Spectral regularization, and learning only the residual of a truncated eigenbasis expansion, corrected much of this error within 500 epochs. The ingredients here, namely Laplacian eigenproblems, basis expansions and learned corrections to them, recur throughout electronic structure. Report.

Hub-Laplacian operators for directional GNNs

Python · PyTorch Geometric · QM9 · with A. Carraro and I. Heijmink (TU Delft)

Integrated degree-biased graph shift operators, fixed or with learnable bias α, into polynomial-filter GCNNs and Graph Anisotropic Diffusion for molecular property regression on QM9. With a well-tuned α they consistently beat standard baselines, which we link to larger spectral gaps. Report.

Chemical potential of a dense Lennard-Jones fluid

Julia · molecular dynamics · free-energy methods

Alchemical particle insertion with a soft-core potential, using Langevin NVT dynamics with a BAOAB integrator. It compares Widom insertion, thermodynamic integration, FEP, BAR and MBAR from the gas phase up to high-density fluids, with uncertainty estimates and tail corrections. Report.
